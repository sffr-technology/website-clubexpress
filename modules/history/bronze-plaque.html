A permanent marker in Golden Gate Park

The Story of the Bronze Plaque

A $5,000 inclusion grant, a year of planning and one small bronze marker gave San Francisco FrontRunners a lasting place in the landscape where the club has gathered for generations.

The plaque is easy to miss. It sits quietly in the top of a green picnic table near the Blue Heron Lake Boathouse, at the place then known as Stow Lake. Yet the small marker represents something much larger: a deliberate effort to make the history and presence of San Francisco’s LGBTQ+ running and walking community visible in public space.

Its story began in May 2019, when International Front Runners invited member clubs to apply for support through a partnership with Brooks Running. The grant asked clubs to propose ways to grow and become more inclusive of LGBT+ runners and walkers. SFFR answered with an idea rooted in both memory and place.

An archival note

The surviving project record says the Brooks grant was $5,000 while the Parks Alliance required a minimum $6,000 contribution for a plaque. It does not explain how the remaining $1,000 was covered.

From invitation to installation

A year in the making

The dated project record reveals how the idea moved from a national grant opportunity to a physical marker in Golden Gate Park.

  1. May 10, 2019

    The opportunity arrives

    Danny Luong, International Front Runners President and Western Region Representative, emailed FrontRunner clubs about a partnership with Brooks Running. Clubs could apply for a $5,000 grant supporting growth and greater inclusion of LGBT+ runners and walkers. The program awarded ten grant packages between May 2019 and December 2020.

  2. July 30, 2019

    SFFR submits its idea

    Bob Callori submitted SFFR’s application to International Front Runners. It described the club’s origins and proposed commemorative plaques for a picnic table and a park bench, supported by photographs of both possible locations.

  3. October 29, 2019

    The grant is approved

    Liem Nguyen received confirmation from Lauren Osborn, Marketing Team Executive Assistant at Brooks, that SFFR’s application had been approved.

  4. January 13, 2020

    The plan meets park requirements

    In a call with Paula Martin, Development Operations Manager at the SF Parks Alliance, Bob learned that the park used standard furniture and that only a plaque was eligible. The Parks Alliance required a minimum $6,000 donation for the plaque.

  5. February 4, 2020

    A location and format take shape

    Bob Callori and Liem Nguyen met with Paula Martin to discuss a 4 by 8-inch bronze plaque for existing park furniture in the grassy area near the Stow Lake Boathouse parking lot.

  6. February 5, 2020

    The club chooses its design

    Bob presented four design options at the club’s monthly meeting. Members approved a final design, which was then submitted to the SF Parks Alliance.

  7. June 2020

    The plaque finds its home

    The finished bronze plaque was installed on a green picnic table near the boathouse parking lot, creating a permanent marker of SFFR’s presence in Golden Gate Park.

The final design

A concise statement of identity

Bob Callori’s original design combined the SFFR logo with the club’s name, its LGBTQ+ identity, the Saturday gathering and the year the community began. The park’s bronze rendering simplified the typography while preserving the message.

San Francisco FrontRunners
LGBTQ+ Run & Walk Club
Meets Every Saturday
Since 1974   SFFR.org

A small marker with a long memory

Part of the landscape

Mounted flush with the tabletop, the plaque does not behave like a monument. It becomes part of an everyday gathering place where people pause, share food and meet before or after time in the park.

That modest placement feels fitting for a club built around showing up week after week. The marker makes SFFR’s history public without separating it from the ordinary community life that history made possible.

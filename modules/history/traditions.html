1998 to today

Traditions & Landmarks

The joyful runs, gathering places and physical markers that have carried the spirit of San Francisco FrontRunners across generations.

1998 01

Golden Gate Park Water Fountain

In 1998 the club installed a water fountain in Golden Gate Park at Transverse Drive and Kennedy Drive. There was concern that explicitly describing the club could invite vandalism, so its bronze plaque reads, “The Running Club for Lesbians, Gay Men and Their Friends.”

Documented as the first Pride Run beneficiary by The Family Link and Friends of Recreation & Parks, it proudly stands untarnished to this day.

1998 02

The Easter Bonnet Run & Contest

The earliest photo found in the Footprint newsletters dates to 1998. John Kruse always showed up in a pink outfit. The first club photos were taken in 2004.

The group ran two miles, similar to the Little Black Dress Run, then had brunch in the park. Chuck Louden and Reggie Snowden often ran the “walkway,” followed by prizes including chocolate bunnies, Easter baskets and stuffed animals. Account by Bob Callori.

1999 03

The Little Black Dress Run

In 1999, Anne Ludwig and Terri Hill of Baylands FrontRunners organized the first Little Black Dress Run. The event grew from a desire to hold a San Francisco event similar to the San Diego Hash House Harriers’ Red Dress Run.

An original logo was designed for the event. The social, leisurely run has continued through the years, and the club carries on the tradition today.

2020 04

The Bronze Plaque at Blue Heron Lake

A 2019 inclusion grant from Brooks Running and International Front Runners began a year-long effort to give SFFR a permanent marker in Golden Gate Park.

The result was a 4 by 8-inch bronze plaque installed in June 2020 on a green picnic table at what was then called Stow Lake.

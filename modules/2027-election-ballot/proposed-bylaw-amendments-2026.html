2026 Board Bulletin

Proposed Bylaw Amendments

From time to time, we revisit our club’s bylaws to ensure they continue to serve us as we grow and technology evolves. The 2026 Board has reviewed the bylaws and is proposing seven amendments to support clearer governance, strengthen club operations, and modernize provisions first drafted more than 40 years ago.

These are the only amendments approved by the Board. They reflect months of input and discussion from the Form 1023 Committee, retained counsel, and the Board.

Read the full proposed amendments Email the Board

Overview

Why the Board is proposing these changes

The proposed amendments clarify SFFR’s mission, establish clearer Board residency and voting standards, update provisions related to the club’s anticipated 501(c)(3) status, and modernize governance and financial procedures.

The summaries below are intended to help members understand each proposal. Members should review the full text of the proposed amendments before voting.

Seven proposals

Summary of proposed amendments

Each proposal will be submitted to the SFFR membership for approval.

1

Modernize SFFR’s mission statement

Clarify that Article 3 reflects SFFR’s mission and make minor changes to modernize the mission statement.

This amendment updates outdated terminology, explicitly recognizes walking, social activities, and community service as important parts of SFFR’s mission, and affirms that the organization welcomes LGBTQ+ individuals and the broader public. It also acknowledges SFFR’s charitable and community involvement, reinforces its nonpartisan character, and preserves running and walking as the organization’s primary focus.

2

Establish a Board residency requirement

Include a residency requirement in the bylaws for members serving on the SFFR Board of Directors.

The current bylaws provide for a Board vacancy when a Director “departs the locality,” but do not define that locality or set objective residency criteria. This amendment defines the locality as the nine Bay Area counties and creates a measurable residency standard for candidates and Board members throughout their terms. The goal is to promote consistent application of the bylaws and keep Board members meaningfully connected to the community SFFR serves.

3

Update the dedication-of-assets provision for 501(c)(3) status

Modify Article XV to specify that SFFR is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization, conditioned on SFFR receiving an IRS determination letter confirming that status.

  1. 2022: SFFR applied to change its tax-exempt status from 501(c)(7) to 501(c)(3) and received a signed IRS letter indicating that SFFR was recognized as a 501(c)(3) organization.
  2. 2025: The Treasurer discovered discrepancies in IRS records, which still listed SFFR as a 501(c)(7). The Board began working with the IRS to resolve the issue.
  3. July 2026: The Board formed a Form 1023 Committee to prepare a new application with guidance from legal counsel, requesting recognition retroactive to the original 2022 application.
  4. 2026: A new Form 1023 was submitted to the IRS.
  5. Now: The Board is proposing this amendment in anticipation of an IRS determination letter confirming 501(c)(3) status.

The amendment provides that, upon dissolution, SFFR’s remaining assets will be distributed to an organization with similar purposes that qualifies under Section 501(c)(3). Until the IRS determination is received, the existing bylaw provision referencing Section 501(c)(7) will remain in effect.

4

Remove the quarterly membership meeting requirement

Amend Article VI.A to remove a quarterly meeting requirement that no longer reflects SFFR’s current practices.

The amendment provides greater flexibility to hold meetings when needed while preserving the annual meeting for elections, organizational updates, awards, and other official business. It reduces administrative obligations without limiting opportunities for member engagement.

5

Clarify voting requirements for members

Amend Article VII.A to ensure all members have an equal opportunity to vote on matters submitted to the membership, whether or not they attend a membership meeting.

The amendment removes outdated distinctions between in-person and absentee voting and gives the Board flexibility to establish procedures that support accessibility, fairness, and member participation.

6

Prohibit compensation for Board members

Add a new Article XIV-K formalizing SFFR’s longstanding practice of having officers serve as volunteers without compensation.

The amendment promotes transparency, accountability, and responsible stewardship of club funds. Board members may still be reimbursed for legitimate club-related expenses incurred while performing official duties.

7

Move financial controls into a Board-maintained policy

Move detailed financial controls from the bylaws into a new Financial Controls Policy. The initial policy would take effect upon membership approval, and future changes would require Board approval and 30 days’ advance notice to members.

The current financial rules can only be updated through a membership vote and no longer reflect all of SFFR’s operational needs. The proposed policy is intended to strengthen safeguards, improve transparency, and make day-to-day procedures more efficient while remaining accessible to all members.

How It All Began

By Bud Budlong

In 1973, San Francisco’s Lavender U. free university invited Jack Baker and Gardner Pond to list a class. The two runners changed their original furniture-refinishing idea into an “introduction to jogging” class, modeled on the scenic runs of the San Francisco Dolphin South End Runners.

The First Run

The first run began near the south windmill in Golden Gate Park on the first Sunday of January 1974. The group became the Lavender U. Joggers and visited scenic locations throughout San Francisco.

Bud Joins

Bud joined in October 1975 and later scheduled and led runs. Attendance grew, and the monthly tailgate brunch became a tradition.

Unique Role

At a time when few LGBTQ+ activities existed outside bars, the club offered community through movement. In 1978, it first staffed a water table at the San Francisco Marathon.

Reinventing Ourselves

After Lavender U. closed in 1978, members reorganized with dues, elected officers, and bylaws. They chose the name Front Runners, inspired by Patricia Nell Warren’s novel. About 40 people became charter members; by the end of 1979 there were 75 members.

The First Officers

The first elected officers were Gary May, president; Pat Stein, vice president; Marvin Shipley, secretary; and Frank Churchill, treasurer. The club became the first gay sports club to join the Amateur Athletic Union.

The First Gay Run

In 1980 the name appeared on a shirt as FrontRunners, one word. That year the club organized the Advocate-sponsored Gay Run, drawing about 400 runners.

The First Other FrontRunner Club

Also in 1980, Malcolm Robinson asked to use the name for Front Runners New York. Other FrontRunner clubs soon formed around the world.

Golden Gate Park Water Fountain

In 1998 the club installed a fountain near Transverse Drive and JFK Drive, with a plaque honoring “The Running Club for Lesbians, Gay Men and Their Friends.”

The Easter Bonnet Run & Contest

Photos of this playful run date to 1998. Members ran two miles, shared brunch, and competed for festive prizes.

The Little Black Dress Run

Anne Ludwig and Terri Hill of Baylands FrontRunners organized the first LBD Run in 1999. The social tradition continues today.

Bronze Plaque

In June 2020 SFFR installed a bronze plaque on a green picnic table at Stow Lake, commemorating a $5,000 award from Brooks Running and International Front Runners.
